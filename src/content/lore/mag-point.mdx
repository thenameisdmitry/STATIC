## The town

> "The first thing you'll notice about Mag Point is the fog. The last thing you'll remember is what you saw inside it."

{/* ============ 1 · ATMOSPHERE ============ */}

Mag Point is a remote coastal town on Baranof Island in Southeast Alaska, a place where 
civilization ends and the wilderness begins. Wedged between towering mountain ridges and the 
icy waters of the Gulf of Alaska, it spends much of the year beneath heavy clouds, 
relentless rain, and endless winter nights. Sunshine is a rare luxury reserved for the brief 
summer months. Thick coastal fog drifts in from the sea throughout the year, but in August it 
becomes almost unnatural, blanketing the town and surrounding forests for days at a time. 
Once a thriving fishing and oil town, Mag Point has spent decades in slow decline. Young people 
leave in search of a better future, businesses struggle to survive, and abandoned buildings 
stand as reminders of more prosperous days. The town has earned a grim reputation among 
outsiders.

<video src="/lore/mag-point/mpvid2.mp4" autoplay muted loop playsinline></video>

Since the late 1980s, Mag Point has been plagued by a series of unexplained disappearances. 
Many dismiss them as unfortunate accidents caused by the unforgiving Alaskan wilderness or 
attribute them to the nearby Alaska Triangle, a region infamous for mysterious vanishings 
and unsolved cases. The greatest tragedy came in August 2008, when five young children 
vanished within days of one another, shattering the community. A decade later, in 2018, the 
disappearances begin again, drawing investigators into 
a mystery that reaches far beyond missing persons. In this story, Mag Point is more than a 
setting. It is a character in its own right: a lonely, weather-beaten town where every 
August the fog returns, bringing with it questions no one has ever been able to answer.



{/* ============ 2 · HISTORY (collapsible) ============ */}

<Collapsible title="History of Mag Point" hint="open file">

<p><b>The Russian Frontier (1805–1867)</b></p>

<p>Mag Point was founded in 1805 by Russian explorer Vasiliy Sokolov. Originally known as 
Fort Mag Point, it served as the easternmost 
military and trading outpost of the Russian-American Company on Baranof Island. 
The settlement occupied a narrow strip of land between steep mountain ridges and the sea, 
making it both naturally protected and strategically important. Ships departing from its 
harbor carried explorers, hunters, missionaries, and supplies deeper into the Alaskan frontier. 
The fort remained small but prosperous, becoming known for its skilled shipwrights, fisheries, 
and timber trade.</p>
<br/>

<p><b>Under American Rule (1867–1897)</b></p>

<p>Following the United States' purchase of Alaska in 1867, Mag Point peacefully transitioned 
to American administration. Many Russian settlers remained, leaving behind an architectural and 
cultural legacy still visible throughout the town. The harbor continued to grow as a regional 
shipping stop, while fishing gradually replaced the fur trade as the town's primary industry.</p>
<br/>

<p><b>The Gold Rush Boom (1897–1915)</b></p>

<p>Although no major gold deposits were discovered near Mag Point itself, the town became an 
important supply port during the Klondike Gold Rush. Hotels, warehouses, saloons, banks, and 
shipping companies appeared almost overnight as thousands of prospectors passed through on 
their way north. At its peak, Mag Point was one of southeastern Alaska's busiest coastal 
settlements. When the Gold Rush ended, many newcomers stayed, helping transform the temporary 
frontier town into a permanent community. </p>
<br/>

<p><b>A Fishing Town (1915–1929)</b></p>

<p>With mining in decline, Mag Point embraced commercial fishing. Large canneries, shipyards, 
and seafood processing facilities were built along the waterfront, while schools, churches, 
parks, and civic buildings reshaped the growing town. The original Russian fort was 
dismantled during this period, with only a monument to its founder, Vasiliy Sokolov, 
remaining in the town square.</p>
<br/>

<p><b>Decline and Recovery (1929–1970)</b></p>

<p>The Great Depression severely affected Mag Point. Businesses closed, unemployment soared, 
and many families left in search of work elsewhere. The town slowly recovered after World War II 
as military contracts, improved infrastructure, and renewed fishing activity brought jobs back 
to the region. New roads, neighborhoods, and public buildings modernized much of the aging 
settlement.</p>
<br/>

<p><b>Oil and Prosperity (1970–1990)</b></p>

<p>The discovery of offshore oil led to another economic boom. "Shoreside Petroleum" established 
operations near Mag Point, creating hundreds of jobs alongside the already thriving fishing 
industry. By the early 1980s, the population had reached nearly 25,000, making Mag Point 
one of the largest towns in southeastern Alaska. Modern schools, a hospital, expanded harbor 
facilities, and private businesses reflected the town's newfound prosperity. Yet during the 
late 1970s, a handful of unusual events began to circulate among locals. Hunters 
occasionally reported strange lights deep in the mountains. Fishermen described hearing 
distant voices over calm waters where no boats were present. Unexplained 
radio interference was dismissed as a natural anomaly, becoming little more than local 
folklore.</p>
<br/>

<p><b>Modern Times (1990–now)</b></p>

<p>By the early 1990s, Mag Point entered a long period of economic decline. Fish stocks 
became less reliable, oil production slowed, and many of the town's largest employers 
began reducing their workforce. Younger generations left for larger Alaskan cities or the 
mainland in search of better opportunities. <br/> <br/>By 2008, the population had fallen from nearly 
25,000 to approximately 15,000, leaving Mag Point struggling to preserve its identity.
Around the same time, unsettling rumors began to spread. A handful of unexplained 
disappearances that had started in the late Cold War years became more frequent, though never 
numerous enough to attract national attention. Local authorities investigated each case 
individually, attributing them to accidents, harsh wilderness conditions, or people leaving 
town without notice. Among residents, however, whispers persisted that something darker had 
taken root in Mag Point. <br/> <br/>Despite its decline, the town remains alive. Its harbor still served a modest fishing fleet, 
"Shoreside Petroleum" maintained regional operations, and family-owned businesses fought 
to survive alongside aging hotels, restaurants, and seafood processing facilities. 
To outsiders, Mag Point was simply another struggling Alaskan coastal town. <br/> <br/>To its 
residents, it was a place that had never quite let go of its past.</p>
